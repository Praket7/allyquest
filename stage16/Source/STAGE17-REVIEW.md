# Ally Quest Stage 17 — Educational, clinical and accessibility qualification

Release candidate based on Stage 16 commit c5ebf1891fac237274e332d153515ae705e7183b. This is a reviewer packet, not a completed human review, medical training or evidence of learning effectiveness. No real food or medication should be used in playtesting. Keep the original Welcome Street campaign and all 11 interiors; the field guide is optional, separate from progression.

## Research and design rationale

- CDC, Food Allergies in Schools (2024): https://www.cdc.gov/school-health-conditions/food-allergies/ — individualized plans, prevention, prepared adult response; FARE classroom toolkit https://www.foodallergy.org/resources/food-allergies-classroom — food-free alternatives, avoid food rewards, soap and water, emergency preparedness.
- FDA, Have Food Allergies? Read the Label: https://www.fda.gov/consumers/consumer-updates/have-food-allergies-read-label — current full ingredient information even for familiar foods; changes occur; labels are not individual approval. FARE https://www.foodallergy.org/resources/how-read-food-label and https://www.foodallergy.org/resources/recognizing-and-responding-reaction — no waiting during possible anaphylaxis, epinephrine first-line, emergency help. These are guidance sources, not clinical validation of this game.
- Mayer & Johnson (2010), Adding Instructional Features That Promote Learning in a Game-Like Environment: https://scispace.com/papers/adding-instructional-features-that-promote-learning-in-a-1thy726g7j — reflection and principle-focused guidance can support transfer in a circuit-learning game; not direct proof for food allergies.
- Jay, Habgood & Mees (2019), Game-based training to promote arithmetic fluency: https://scispace.com/papers/game-based-training-to-promote-arithmetic-fluency-2t2tmqtvnx — example of measuring learning outside the game; different content domain.
- Brom, van Gog & Děchtěrenko (2026), Retrieval Practice in the Wild: https://scispace.com/papers/retrieval-practice-in-the-wild-comparing-different-multiple-fqykoyxyt0eu — learner-paced presentation showed small persistence effects, not improved short-term learning. No mandatory delays, no grades or time pressure in check-ins.
- W3C WCAG 2.2 https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/ — keyboard access, focus visibility, dragging alternatives, target size and status announcements. The room object chooser invokes existing interaction functions rather than awarding completion. Not a claim of full conformance or screen-reader accessibility of platforming.
- TaskAudit (2025 preprint) https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2510.12972 and Human-AI Collaborative Game Testing with Vision Language Models (2025 preprint) https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2501.11782 — test actual flows and retain human evaluation; not proof that automation can replace children or clinical reviewers.

## Objective and scenario traceability matrix

| Room | Objective | Correct observable behavior | Adversarial misconception to probe |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kitchen | Ask, labels, wash | Consult responsible adult, hold changed delivery, practice soap/water, adult review | Familiar or tiny taste is safe; completed simulation authorizes eating |
| Washroom | Contact, wash | Separate used utensils, stop using contacted rack, adult-directed cleaning | Visible cleanliness or sanitizer proves allergen removal |
| Mural | Include, ask | Separate cereal and offer reviewed non-food supplies | Art food ingredients never matter |
| Milk lab | Labels, ask | Identify milk on current label, report to nurse | Same brand means same ingredients |
| Missing-details lab | Labels, ask | Hold incomplete information and ask adult | No warning means no risk |
| Recipe-change lab | Labels | Compare current soy listing with old recipe, request review | Reuse previous approval |
| Garden | Include, labels | Hold unknown craft dough, arrange reviewed paper alternative | All craft supplies are food-allergen-free |
| Music | Contact, wash, include | Keep snacks away from instruments, adult-directed cleaning, handwashing | Sharing equipment after eating is always harmless |
| Welcome studio | Include | Invite without pressure or health disclosure | Friend must eat or explain diagnosis |
| Clue clubhouse | Include, ask | Arrange non-food prizes; no food trading | Mystery food can be shared if well intended |
| Festival | Emergency, include | Recognize throat tightness as urgent, alert adult / emergency help immediately | Finish a task, inspect labels, or wait for a rash first |

Check-in forms A/B each cover six domains, with distinct situations and balanced form assignment. Practice uses separate prompts (C) and immediate explanatory feedback. First answers are immutable until local deletion. Scores are not mastery certification. Form equivalence, reliability, readability, sensitivity to learning, and delayed transfer have NOT been established. Do not compare scores from learners who did not follow the protocol. The optional app permits skipping and does not enforce study ordering; facilitator does that. No timing, medical information, identity, telemetry, or network export is collected. Anonymous responses may still be identifiable if linked to a study roster; keep any linkage separately controlled by the responsible institution, not this game.

## Independent clinical review — before recruiting children

Recruit a licensed allergist and school nurse (or other appropriately qualified school health professional); ideally each reviews independently. Provide exact build SHA, incident script, objectives, all 18 question prompts and test cases. Use synthetic scenarios only. Rate each matrix row: accurate / needs clarification / unsafe / not evaluated; record evidence, proposed correction and reviewer/date outside the game. Review emergency language for local procedures and age-appropriate child role: immediate adult and EMS activation, prompt epinephrine according to emergency plan, no delaying for investigation, no requirement for rash, no reassurance from absent signs. Review washing and cross-contact, hidden ingredients, labeling exceptions, nondisclosure, inclusive language, and disability access. Review both branches and incorrect actions; no child should infer the game certifies food. A single unsafe or ambiguous emergency/food-approval item blocks release until correction and independent re-review. Do not represent published guidance review as signed specialist approval.

## Formative child usability pilot (proposed, not performed)

1. Obtain school/organization approval, guardian permission and age-appropriate assent; participation voluntary, no adverse consequences for stopping. If intended as generalizable research or for a competition, seek the applicable institutional/IRB or fair approval before recruiting; follow school privacy policies. Do not ask about a child's diagnosis, prescribed medications or real emergency history. Do not use real food, allergens or medication. Have a trained adult present. Stop if distressed; use fictional examples only.
2. Suggested initial design: two iterative small formative rounds, 5–8 participants each in the intended age band (proposed ages 8–12, confirm with educators); include varied input preferences and accessibility needs with accommodations, not diagnoses. Also independently test adults/teachers and users of assistive technologies. Small formative samples find issues, not population-level effect sizes. Choose devices the school actually uses. Record version, device category, observed task success, facilitator assistance level, and anonymous error codes; no names, videos, audio or exact ages unless separately approved and necessary. Retain only as authorized; delete local responses between participants after approved export.
3. Neutral moderator script: “This is a fictional game. We are testing the game, not you. You may stop at any time. Show me what you think you would do.” Avoid leading questions. Before game/guide exposure, administer six first-response check-in items; do not reveal answers. Play the campaign or the prespecified scenario subset with equal exposure. Record unassisted vs assisted outcomes, blocked actions, misinterpretations, frustration, completion and voluntary replay interest. Then administer alternate-form post-check, before guide practice/feedback. Use separate novel oral transfer vignettes 7–14 days later if approved and feasible; do not repeat app items or give unsafe instructions as practice. Do not encourage tasting or medical action in real life.
4. Delayed transfer examples for facilitator (free response): (a) a new supplier brings muffins with an incomplete label; (b) a used spatula rests on another tray; (c) someone washes with water only before using communal art tools; (d) a classmate wants to attend a party without eating; (e) a fictional peer develops breathing trouble after eating; (f) a familiar sauce has changed ingredients. Ask “What would you do and why?” Accept correct principles expressed in the learner's own words. Score 0 unsafe/unclear, 1 partially correct, 2 correct plus rationale; for emergency require immediate help, no delay, no puzzle, no treatment misinformation. Two independent raters review deidentified responses, adjudicate disagreements; record agreement. This is a proposed rubric, not validated.
5. Suggested provisional release gates: zero critical safety misunderstandings attributable to instructional wording (any triggers revision, not blame); 100% of safety-critical test routes maintain adult review; at least 80% unassisted completion of core actions in a usability pilot, and report assistance/dropouts; no keyboard trap or lost focus blocking tasks. Report item-level correct counts and paired change only among eligible complete pairs; report missing data and uncertainty. Do not declare efficacy from an uncontrolled pre/post pilot. A larger appropriately approved controlled evaluation with delayed transfer and preregistered outcomes is required for causal claims. Record engagement separately from learning.

## Accessibility and physical device qualification

Test actual iPad Safari, iPhone Safari, Android Chrome, school Chromebook and desktop keyboard; include a real screen reader (VoiceOver, NVDA or TalkBack), keyboard-only and switch-compatible inputs where relevant. Check focus order, readable labels, focus recovery, target sizing, small-screen text wrapping, enlarged text and zoom, color independence, motion and audio options. Confirm object list, carry/place, gesture alternative, dialogue, immediate emergency button, all exits and menu recovery; outdoor platforming remains a visual motor task, not proven accessible to blind players. Use the Stage 16 device profiler for 30s samples on representative hardware; record device class, browser, orientation, median/p95 frame interval, dropped frames, input response, thermal issues and any bugs. Compare proposed median 33.3ms and p95 50ms for interiors, target 16.7ms desktop; treat these as engineering targets, not medical/usability standards. No physical devices or human participants were available in this session.
